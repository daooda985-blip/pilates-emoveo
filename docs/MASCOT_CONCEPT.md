# emoveo — mascot concept and behavior (working brief)

## Status
The mascot is an approved product direction. Its final name, species/form, silhouette, personality details and visual design are **not approved yet**. They must be developed gradually with the user. Do not treat “Mori” or the existing seed/petal SVG as a final decision; both are temporary explorations.

## Confirmed product behavior

### 1. Supportive relationship with daily activity
- The mascot is connected to the user's daily activity and Pilates practice.
- It should encourage the user to return to practice.
- It must not shame, punish, guilt-trip or scold the user for missed days.
- A break in activity should not be framed as failure or loss.

### 2. Practice together
- During a workout, the mascot trains alongside the user.
- It mirrors the movements in the generated Pilates routine.
- The mascot's movement should correspond to the actual exercise sequence, not be a generic looping animation.
- A completed full workout can contribute to visible changes in the mascot's body/form.

### 3. Development and personalization
- The approved direction combines two systems:
  1. **Automatic development:** the mascot changes over time in response to completed practice — body/form, movement abilities and emotional expression can evolve.
  2. **User customization:** the user can personalize the mascot's appearance, clothing and accessories.
- Automatic development and user customization are complementary, not competing systems.
- Do not define exact stages, thresholds, attributes or visual transformations until designed with the user.

### 4. Gifts and streaks
- A streak of 3 or more consecutive active days can unlock gifts for the mascot.
- Example gift category: new looks/appearance options.
- Gifts should feel like positive surprises, not compensation for punishment.
- The precise streak rules, reset behavior and reward catalogue remain to be designed.

## Experience principles
- Encouragement without pressure.
- Progress without shame.
- The mascot accompanies the user; it does not judge the user.
- Visual evolution should communicate practice and care, not body ideals or weight-loss goals.
- The mascot should remain optional and should not obstruct the workout interface.

## Gradual design process

### Stage 1 — character concept (not yet decided)
Discuss the mascot's role, emotional tone, degree of abstraction and relationship to Pilates / movement. Do not lock a name or appearance.

### Stage 2 — name
Explore name directions only after the character concept has enough shape. “Mori” is an unconfirmed working suggestion, not the selected name.

### Stage 3 — visual identity
Develop several distinct silhouettes and visual treatments. Select one with the user. The existing SVG is only a placeholder study and must not be considered approved artwork.

### Stage 4 — expressions and movement
Define a restrained set of states (for example: greeting, accompanying practice, encouragement, rest/return) and how the character mirrors exercises. Final states should follow the approved character design.

### Stage 5 — progression and rewards
Specify how completed workouts affect development, how 3+ day streak gifts work, and how customization interacts with automatic evolution.

### Stage 6 — implementation
Only after the above decisions, build the character system and connect it to workout events. Do not add streak tracking, reward persistence, progression or exercise animation as if already implemented.
